Grace, Underlined Twice

You kept the world indoors when you were small,
watchful, quiet, ambitious in the dark —
already dreaming bigger than us all,
already carrying an outsized spark.

Then somewhere past the hallways of sixteen
the quiet girl walked out and lit the room.
The introvert stayed close, still lives between
the version loud with love, and one with room
to breathe alone before she gives again —
and Ann, you give so easily it hurts.
Sweets for the watchmen, kindness with no end,
a softness that no small talk ever curbs.

You'll stop a lesson for a grasshopper,
insist the mirror's purely functional,
tell stories like a river finds the sea —
slow, winding, never in a straight line,
and I have learned to love the wandering,
because the getting-there is always fine
when it is you who's doing the telling.

You sign your name in capitals, all bold,
as if the letters knew what I'd find out:
that grace, the meaning your own name holds,
was never soft-spoken or lived in doubt.
Stargazer lilies, Mailo at your feet,
a violin you don't play anymore
but still plays you — precise, and quietly sweet,
thorough in loving like you've never before.

I'm writing this so some part of today
outlives today. So if you ever ask
what I saw first, and what made me stay —
it's this: you turn the ordinary task
of living into something generous and true.
Two minutes late, and worth the wait, all through.
And if my heart could sign the way you do,
it would write, unshaking, all in caps: I love you.
